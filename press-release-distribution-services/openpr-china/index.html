What you buy

A public posting, internationally visible

openPR accepts press release submissions and publishes them as public pages on its platform, with paid options for enhanced exposure. Its international framing reflects the platform's language reach, not local media relationships in the markets it names.

The distinction that matters for China buyers is between public posting and editorial coverage. A public posting means anyone can find the page if they look. Editorial coverage means a Chinese media organization published the release on its own website under its own standards. openPR produces the first; China goals usually require the second.

What to verify

Two checks for China buyers

  1. Classify the result correctly. An openPR page in a report should be labeled a self-publishing platform entry — the page-type discipline from what a coverage report should show.
  2. Do not let free platforms carry China KPIs. If a low-cost package includes openPR-style pages in a "China media" count, apply the checks in the fake placement guide.

Does openPR publish on Chinese media websites?

No. openPR publishes releases on its own platform. Its international scope refers to platform language reach, not placement on Chinese media websites.

What is the difference between a public posting and editorial coverage?

A public posting is self-published content on a platform page. Editorial coverage is publication by a media organization under its editorial standards, on its own domain. Only the second carries third-party media credibility.

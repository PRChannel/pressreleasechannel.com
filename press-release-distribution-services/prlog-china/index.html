Provider review

PRLog for China

PRLog publishes press releases as public pages on its own platform, free with paid upgrades; it is a useful public information node, not a channel for Chinese media placement.

What you buy

A public page on PRLog's own domain, with optional extras

PRLog's free tier publishes the release as a public page on prlog.org; paid upgrades add features such as broader distribution and placement boosts. Nothing about the model involves Chinese media: the publishing environment is PRLog's own website, and its audience is English-speaking.

Used honestly, the free tier has a legitimate role: it creates an indexable, permanent public record of an announcement. That record can be cited, linked and archived. What it cannot do is appear on a Chinese media website or reach Chinese readers through Chinese channels.

What to verify

Two checks for China buyers

  1. Never count a PRLog page as China coverage. A prlog.org URL is PR content on a US platform. The rule for what counts is in what a China coverage report should actually show.
  2. Beware the "200 outlets for $50" category. Free and low-cost self-publishing platforms are the padding inside padded media lists. If a cheap package cites platforms like this as "media placements," it matches the pattern in the fake placement guide.
When this model fits

A free record, and nothing more

For startups with no budget, PRLog's free page is a reasonable public record. For any buyer with China goals, the correct use is as an archive footnote at most — the China layer must come from elsewhere.

Is PRLog useful for reaching Chinese audiences?

No. PRLog publishes on its own English-language platform. Chinese audiences discover content through Chinese platforms and search, which PRLog pages do not participate in.

Why do padded media lists include platforms like PRLog?

Because they are free or nearly free and produce real URLs, which makes a report look large at almost no cost. The pages are real; the "media placement" framing is not.

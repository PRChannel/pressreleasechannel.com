> **Disclosure:** This review is based solely on publicly available information. PR Channel was not involved in Laifen's IFA 2026 campaign unless explicitly stated. The purpose is to examine communication structure, evidence quality and public information consistency rather than to evaluate Laifen's products or commercial performance.

## The communication question

Laifen arrived at IFA 2026 with a broader story than a single product launch.

The central communication challenge was clear: how does a company that became widely associated with high-speed hair dryers show that it is becoming a broader personal care technology brand?

A press release alone cannot prove that transition. The claim becomes more credible when several public signals support the same direction:

- a broader product portfolio;
- an official trade-show presence;
- independently verifiable awards or editorial recognition;
- real retail availability;
- repeated product and market information across first-party and third-party sources.

This is what makes Laifen's IFA 2026 communication useful as a public case study.

## 1. The message was not simply "we launched new products"

The international announcement around IFA 2026 presented a portfolio that extended across hair care, beauty, grooming and oral care.

Public materials referenced products including the AutoCurl Curling Iron, Glowy Vanity Mirror and Wave Lite Electric Toothbrush, alongside existing hair dryers, electric toothbrushes and shavers.

The more important message was therefore not the number of products.

It was the intended brand transition:

**from a company strongly associated with high-speed hair dryers to a broader personal care technology brand.**

For an international audience, that is a stronger communication frame than treating each product as an isolated release.

## 2. IFA acted as a credibility anchor

Trade shows can become useful communication assets when the event itself is independently verifiable.

IFA's public exhibitor information listed Laifen at Hall 9, booth H9-125. IFA's 2026 Innovation Awards also listed Laifen Neo Special as an honoree in Beauty Tech & Wellbeing.

Those facts matter because they are not simply claims repeated on the brand's own website.

They provide external reference points that can support the broader story.

A company can say it is expanding into new categories, but an official trade-show presence and a product recognized within the show's own award system give that statement a stronger public evidence layer.

## 3. Different evidence types should not be treated as equal

One of the most common weaknesses in international brand communication is compressing every positive signal into the same phrase, such as "international recognition."

That removes useful distinctions.

For a communication review, the evidence is better separated into categories.

### Trade-show evidence

IFA exhibitor and award pages can verify participation, product presence and official recognition.

### Brand-issued information

Laifen's own website and press materials can verify what the company says about its portfolio, positioning and planned market activity.

### Distribution-wire content

A PR Newswire release can establish the public version of the company's announcement and the messages the brand intended to distribute internationally.

### Retail evidence

Public product listings at retailers can help verify that a product or brand is actually available through particular sales channels.

### Editorial or independent media evidence

Independent reviews, tests, selections or editorial coverage can provide a different level of validation from company-issued content.

These categories can all be useful, but they prove different things.

For overseas communication teams, keeping those distinctions visible makes a story more defensible.

## 4. Product expansion became more credible when retail channels were visible

A global expansion claim is stronger when audiences can see where products are actually sold.

Laifen's IFA 2026 press materials referenced retail and e-commerce relationships in Europe, including Boots, Costco and MediaMarkt.

That matters because retail availability is a more concrete signal than a general statement such as "expanding across Europe."

A useful communication structure is:

**brand claim → public product evidence → third-party retail availability**

This does not prove commercial success by itself, but it does make the market-expansion story more verifiable.

## 5. China-facing and international communication do not need to use the same narrative

One useful lesson from this case is that a brand does not need to tell exactly the same story in every market.

For a Chinese audience, the communication can emphasize strategic change:

- moving beyond a single hero category;
- expanding the product portfolio;
- building a broader international brand position.

For an overseas audience, the story may work better when it is more concrete:

- which products are launching;
- where they can be seen;
- which retailers carry them;
- what the trade-show experience includes;
- which claims can be independently checked.

That difference is not necessarily inconsistency.

It can be good localization, provided the underlying facts remain aligned.

## 6. Strong claims still require precise evidence

This is where communication teams need discipline.

Terms such as:

- world No. 1;
- first;
- leading;
- fastest-growing;
- preferred by younger consumers;
- globally recognized;

can be useful only when the underlying source, category, period and measurement are clear.

For example, IFA's exhibitor profile currently describes Laifen as recognized by Euromonitor International as the world's No. 1 high-speed hair dryer brand.

That is a stronger formulation when the original certification scope is preserved than when it is simplified into a much broader claim such as "the world's No. 1 personal care brand."

The lesson is simple:

**the stronger the claim, the more specific the evidence should be.**

## 7. What was strong about the communication structure

From a public-information perspective, several elements worked together well.

### The brand shift had a visible product basis

The broader personal care positioning was supported by multiple product categories rather than by a slogan alone.

### The trade-show context was verifiable

IFA provided an external event context and official exhibitor information.

### The message connected products with channels

The communication did not stop at product announcements; retail availability helped connect the story to real market access.

### Multiple public sources reinforced the same direction

Brand pages, event pages, wire-distributed releases and other third-party references collectively made the broader brand transition easier to understand.

## 8. What could be clearer

The main risk is not a lack of information.

It is the tendency to place different types of evidence into one undifferentiated category of "international recognition."

A more mature communication structure would make the source type explicit.

For example:

- **Official trade-show recognition**
- **Independent editorial review**
- **Commercial partnership or sponsored content**
- **Retail availability**
- **Company-issued market data**
- **Third-party market research**

This makes the communication more transparent and easier to verify.

It also reduces the risk that a reader interprets all external references as equally independent.

## 9. What overseas companies can learn from this case

This case is useful beyond Laifen.

For any company communicating across China and international markets, five practical lessons stand out.

### Build the story around a business change

A product launch is stronger when audiences understand what is changing in the company or market.

### Use external events as evidence, not decoration

Trade shows can support credibility when participation, products and recognition are publicly verifiable.

### Separate evidence by type

Retail pages, awards, editorial reviews and company statements should not be presented as if they were interchangeable.

### Show market access where possible

A product listed by a real retailer can be more useful than a vague statement about geographic expansion.

### Keep facts aligned across markets

Different markets can receive different narratives, but the underlying company facts should remain consistent.

## Final observation

The most interesting part of Laifen's IFA 2026 communication was not that the company appeared at a major trade show.

It was that several public information layers pointed in the same direction:

**broader product portfolio → trade-show presence → external recognition → retail availability → international brand positioning**

That structure makes a brand transition easier for media, partners, search engines and AI systems to understand.

For communications teams, the lesson is not to reproduce Laifen's exact campaign.

It is to ask a more useful question:

> **What public evidence would make our own brand story independently understandable and verifiable?**

A recognizable media name can be useful, but it does not tell you everything about a placement.

In China, the same media brand may appear across different publishing environments. For an overseas company, this can make two placements with similar names look equivalent even when they are not.

## The media brand is only one layer

A placement should be evaluated at several levels:

- the media brand;
- the actual domain;
- the page type;
- the publishing account;
- the regional or national scope;
- whether recommendation is included.

The brand name alone cannot answer all of these questions.

## Main-site pages and content accounts are different

Some media organizations operate main news sites and also maintain content accounts on larger platforms. A page published through a content account may display a familiar publisher name while existing in a different technical and editorial environment.

That does not automatically make the page invalid. It does mean the placement should be described accurately.

## Regional does not always mean national

A media brand can have regional sections or local channels. These may be useful for location-specific stories, but they should not be presented as identical to a national placement.

## Recommendation is a separate layer

Publication and recommendation are different events. An article can be live without being recommended on a homepage or in an app.

If recommendation is sold as part of a package, the verification method should be clear.

## The actual domain matters

The live URL is one of the easiest ways to understand the placement. It helps distinguish the public page from a screenshot, sales label or brand logo.

## A better five-question check

Before comparing two media placements, ask:

1. What is the actual domain?
2. What type of page will be published?
3. Is it national, regional or account-based?
4. Is recommendation included, and how is it verified?
5. Will I receive a public URL after publication?

The useful question is not simply, “Is this a big media name?”

It is: **“What type of placement am I actually getting?”**

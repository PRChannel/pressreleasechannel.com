Press release distribution can create public information about a company, but publication should not be confused with guaranteed Google ranking or AI visibility.

For overseas companies, it is useful to separate several stages.

## Stage 1: Publication

Publication means a public media page exists.

This can normally be verified through:

- the live URL;
- the domain;
- the page type;
- the published content.

At this stage, the information is public, but that does not mean every search engine or AI system has discovered it.

## Stage 2: Crawling, indexing or retrieval

Search engines decide whether to crawl and index pages.

AI systems may use different retrieval methods and source sets.

A page can be publicly accessible and still:

- remain unindexed;
- appear only for some queries;
- be discovered slowly;
- be ignored by a particular AI system.

Publication creates the possibility of retrieval. It does not guarantee retrieval.

## Stage 3: Citation

Even if a system retrieves a page, it may not cite that page visibly.

The system may:

- use another source;
- combine several sources;
- answer without showing citations;
- decide the page is not relevant enough to the question.

Citation is therefore a separate outcome.

## Stage 4: Inclusion in the final answer

A brand may be present in a source but still not appear in the final generated answer.

This can happen because:

- the question asks for a different type of information;
- stronger or more independent sources exist;
- the company information is inconsistent;
- the system does not consider the brand relevant enough;
- the retrieved passage does not contain the needed fact.

This is why “the article was published” and “the AI recommended the brand” should not be treated as the same result.

## What press release distribution can contribute

Press release distribution can still be useful for search and AI visibility because it can create:

- public references;
- consistent company facts;
- dated announcements;
- descriptions of products or services;
- evidence that a company participated in an event or partnership;
- additional pages where a brand and a topic appear together.

These can become part of the public information environment around a company.

But the value depends on the quality and consistency of the information, not only the number of pages.

## More pages do not automatically mean better GEO

Publishing the same claim on many low-value pages does not necessarily make an AI system trust or use that claim.

Useful factors can include:

- clear entity names;
- consistent facts;
- relevant context;
- independent references;
- accessible pages;
- topical relevance;
- corroboration across different sources.

This is why brand facts should remain consistent across the company website, media coverage and external profiles.

## A practical measurement framework

Instead of asking only “Did GEO work?”, track four stages:

### 1. Publication
Does the public page exist?

### 2. Retrieval
Can the page or brand be found for relevant queries?

### 3. Citation
Does the system visibly use the page as a source?

### 4. Answer
Does the final answer mention the company or the fact you expected?

Tracking these separately makes it easier to identify where the information chain breaks.

## What should not be promised

Press release distribution should not be sold as a guarantee of:

- Google indexing;
- Google ranking;
- AI citation;
- AI recommendation;
- inclusion in a generated answer.

Those systems make independent decisions.

A more realistic goal is to build a clearer, more consistent and more verifiable public information environment.

Press release distribution can be one part of that environment, especially when the announcement contains real facts that are relevant to the company's long-term public identity.

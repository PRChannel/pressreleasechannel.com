Choosing a press release distribution service in China is difficult if the comparison is based only on media logos, package size or the lowest quoted price.

A more useful comparison looks at how the provider explains the publishing environment and whether the final result can be independently verified.

## Start with the provider's definition of “distribution”

Ask what the service actually includes.

Does the provider:

- recommend relevant media;
- submit the release for editorial review;
- provide writing or translation;
- explain the expected page type;
- provide live URLs after publication;
- coordinate journalists for events;
- monitor or discuss post-publication visibility?

The word “distribution” can cover very different workflows.

## Ask how media are selected

A good media list should reflect the announcement.

For example, a regional manufacturing expansion may need a different mix from a consumer product launch or a technology announcement.

Useful selection factors include:

- subject matter;
- target audience;
- geography;
- industry relevance;
- page type;
- editorial fit.

A provider that only sells large generic media lists may be less useful when the company needs targeted visibility.

## Look beyond recognizable media names

A familiar media brand does not automatically tell you what type of page will be published.

Ask for:

- the actual domain;
- a recent example;
- the expected page type;
- whether the placement is national, regional or account-based;
- whether recommendation is included.

This helps reduce the risk of buying a placement based on a brand label that does not fully describe the final page.

## Check whether editorial review is explained clearly

A legitimate distribution provider should be able to explain that media outlets may retain editorial control.

Depending on the outlet, a release can be:

- accepted;
- edited;
- delayed;
- rejected.

Be cautious when every placement is described as guaranteed without explaining what publishing environment makes that guarantee possible.

## Verify the final result

After publication, you should normally receive a public URL.

The URL allows you to check:

- domain;
- page type;
- headline;
- accessibility;
- company facts;
- publication status.

If verification depends only on screenshots, ask why a public page cannot be provided.

## Separate publication from recommendation

Some offers combine terms such as:

- publication;
- homepage recommendation;
- app recommendation;
- featured placement;
- large reading volume.

These are not the same outcome.

If recommendation is part of the service, ask how it is verified and how long it is expected to remain visible.

## Understand the provider's support policy

Media pages are not always permanent.

Ask:

- whether there is a stated support period;
- what happens if a page disappears;
- whether the provider will investigate or replace a failed placement;
- whether special categories of content have different risks.

A written, understandable support policy is more useful than a vague promise that a page will remain online forever.

## Ask what the provider does not guarantee

Clear service boundaries are a positive signal.

Press release distribution does not normally guarantee:

- editorial acceptance;
- permanent page availability;
- Google indexing;
- high search ranking;
- AI citation;
- recommendation in an AI answer.

A provider that distinguishes these outcomes is easier to evaluate than one that presents them as a single result.

## Check whether the company itself is verifiable

For overseas buyers, it can also help to check the service provider's own public information.

Look for:

- a clear company or brand name;
- an identifiable operator;
- a working website;
- public contact details;
- consistent service descriptions;
- external profiles or references where available.

This does not prove service quality by itself, but it makes the business easier to verify.

## A practical checklist

Before choosing a China press release distribution provider, ask:

1. What exactly is included?
2. How are media selected?
3. What types of pages are expected?
4. Does editorial review apply?
5. Will I receive public URLs?
6. Are recommendation claims verifiable?
7. What support period applies?
8. Are search or AI outcomes guaranteed?
9. Can the provider itself be independently verified?

The best provider is not necessarily the one with the largest media list.

For many overseas companies, the more useful provider is the one that makes the distribution process understandable before the money is paid.

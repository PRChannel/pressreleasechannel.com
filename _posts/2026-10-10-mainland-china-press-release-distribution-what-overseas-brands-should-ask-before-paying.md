Buying press release distribution in Mainland China can be difficult for an overseas brand because much of the media environment is unfamiliar.

A provider may show a large media list, recognizable names and an impressive distribution number. Before paying, ask questions that reveal what the service actually includes.

## 1. What exactly is being distributed?

Ask whether the original English release will be used, whether Chinese translation is included, whether localization is included, who approves changes and whether promotional claims are reviewed before submission.

## 2. Which media categories are included?

Do not ask only which media names are included. Ask what types of media are included: national, regional, industry, business, consumer, technology, finance, local news, content accounts or syndicated pages.

## 3. What are the actual publishing domains?

Ask to see recent examples.

The media name and the publishing domain are not always the same thing. A recent live URL can show where the content actually appears, what the page looks like and whether the format matches your expectations.

## 4. Is publication guaranteed?

If editorial review is involved, publication cannot always be guaranteed.

Ask what happens if the media requests edits, changes the headline, rejects unsupported claims or declines the release entirely.

The replacement or refund policy should be clear before payment.

## 5. Are recommendation claims separately verifiable?

A published article and a recommended article are different things.

If the package includes homepage recommendation, app recommendation, featured placement or high-traffic exposure, ask how those claims are verified.

## 6. What will the final report contain?

At minimum, ask for media name, live URL and publication date.

A stronger report may also contain page type, media category, publication status, accessibility notes and distinctions between editorial and syndicated placements.

## 7. What support is included after publication?

Ask whether there is a defined support period, what happens if a page disappears shortly after publication and whether replacement is possible.

Avoid assuming that every media page will remain online permanently.

## 8. Does the provider guarantee search or AI visibility?

Be cautious if the answer is yes.

Publication does not automatically guarantee Google indexing, Baidu indexing, ranking, AI retrieval or AI citation.

## 9. Is the media plan relevant to the announcement?

Ask why each media category is being recommended. A provider should be able to explain the logic.

## 10. What is the total cost actually buying?

Separate the quote into translation, localization, media distribution, editorial coordination, reporting, after-publication support and account management.

This makes it easier to compare providers fairly.

## Final thought

The biggest risk in Mainland China press release distribution is not always choosing the “wrong media.” It is buying a service without understanding what the deliverables actually mean.

A transparent provider should make the media, page types, URLs, editorial process and support boundaries easier to understand.

For companies planning an event in China, one question often comes up: should we invite journalists, or is press release distribution enough?

The answer depends on whether the event itself can create something worth reporting.

## Invite journalists when the event creates new information

Media invitation makes more sense when something genuinely new is happening. Examples can include a product launch, major partnership, signing ceremony, research announcement, factory opening or industry forum.

If the event only repeats information that is already available, a press release may be more efficient.

## Invite journalists when there is something worth interviewing

One of the main advantages of on-site media attendance is the possibility of creating information that does not already exist in the prepared materials.

Journalists may ask why the company is making a decision now, what has changed in the market or what evidence supports the announcement.

If executives, researchers, technical experts or partners can provide useful answers, the event can create stronger reporting opportunities.

## Visual and on-site value also matters

Exhibitions, product demonstrations, manufacturing site visits, store openings and corporate open days can be easier to understand in person than through a written release alone.

## Attendance does not guarantee coverage

A journalist may attend and still decide not to publish. Coverage depends on news value, audience relevance, editorial judgment, competing news and the quality of the available information.

Media invitation creates an opportunity for coverage. It is not a guarantee of coverage.

## Media invitation and press release distribution solve different problems

Press release distribution starts with information that is already prepared for publication.

Media invitation creates the possibility of additional reporting through observation, interviews and on-site interaction.

Some events benefit from both, but a company does not always need both.

## A simple decision test

Before inviting journalists, ask:

1. Is there a real news development?
2. Is there something worth seeing on-site?
3. Are there people worth interviewing?
4. Is the event relevant to a specific media audience?
5. Can journalists obtain information beyond the prepared release?

If most answers are yes, media invitation may be worth considering.

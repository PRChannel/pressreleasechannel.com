A China PR agency is a communications firm that manages ongoing media relations, strategy and campaigns inside the Chinese market, while a China press release distribution service is a product provider that places individual announcements on Chinese media and delivers verifiable publishing URLs. The difference is not quality — it is whether you are buying a relationship or buying a result.

Overseas companies often search for a "China PR agency" when what their announcement actually needs is the second thing. This guide separates the two, explains what each model can and cannot produce, and gives a decision framework for choosing.

## What a China PR agency actually sells

A full-service agency in China typically sells capacity and counsel:

* brand and messaging strategy for the Chinese market
* ongoing media relations and journalist outreach
* campaign planning across media, social and KOL channels
* crisis communications preparation and response
* localization of materials beyond a single release
* event and media invitation support, such as [media invitations in China](/media-invitation-china/)

The engagement is usually retainer-based, measured in months, and priced accordingly. What the client is buying is a team inside the market.

## What a distribution service actually sells

A distribution service sells a defined, repeatable outcome:

* the press release placed on selected Chinese media
* editorial submission handled locally
* actual publishing URLs delivered after distribution
* placement-type classification and a [coverage report](/china-media-guide/what-a-china-media-coverage-report-should-actually-show/)

The engagement is transactional — per release or per package — and produces auditable evidence. This is the model behind [China press release distribution](/press-release-distribution-china/) as a product category.

## The decision framework

Three questions separate the needs cleanly:

1. **Is the need continuous or announcement-based?** An ongoing China market entry needs agency capacity. A product launch, funding news or executive announcement needs distribution.
2. **Is the deliverable coverage or capability?** If the success criterion is "verifiable articles on Chinese media by date X," that is a distribution product. If it is "build our position in the Chinese market over the year," that is agency work.
3. **Who verifies the result?** Both models should produce verifiable output — the difference is that distribution services are structurally easier to audit, because every placement resolves to a URL. When evaluating either, use the same standard: [verify the placement before you pay](/china-media-guide/how-to-verify-a-chinese-media-placement-before-you-pay/).

A common and efficient pattern for overseas companies is a hybrid: use a distribution service for announcement-based coverage, and add agency support only when a specific continuous need — localization depth, crisis readiness, sustained journalist relations — actually appears.

## How to evaluate a China PR agency before signing

If the agency route is the right one, evaluate it with the same discipline:

* **Published work, not client logos.** Ask for recent placements with live URLs and check the [domains and page types](/china-media-guide/how-to-tell-whether-a-chinese-media-placement-is-actually-valuable/) yourself.
* **China-based team, named.** Confirm which people are actually in China and doing the media work — not a local sales office forwarding to an overseas team.
* **Written deliverables and reporting.** Retainer work drifts without defined outputs; agree on what each month produces.
* **Auditable media claims.** Any media relationship claim should survive the same checks as a purchased placement. Claims that resist verification follow the patterns in [fake media placement and media list scams](/china-media-guide/fake-media-placement-china-media-list-scams/).

## The bottom line

"China PR agency" and "China press release distribution" overlap in language but not in product. Agencies sell ongoing capability; distribution services sell verifiable announcement coverage. Matching the purchase to the actual need — and applying URL-level verification to either — prevents both overspending on retainers for what a single release achieves, and under-delivering on a market entry that genuinely needed agency capacity.

When buying Chinese media distribution, overseas companies often receive a media list before publication.

The list may contain well-known media names. That is useful, but it is not enough.

The most important evidence comes after publication: **the real publishing URL**.

## A media name is a label; a URL is evidence

A media brand may operate across a main website, regional sections, subdomains, content accounts, mobile environments, partner systems and other publishing platforms.

If the buyer only sees the brand name, it may be impossible to know which environment the release will appear in.

The URL shows the actual domain, page, article structure and whether the page is publicly accessible.

## URLs make internal verification easier

Overseas communications teams often need to report results to headquarters, marketing, legal, management, investors or regional teams.

A live URL allows colleagues to verify the result directly. This is especially useful when the person approving the campaign does not read Chinese or know the Chinese media landscape.

## The domain can reveal the real placement type

A media package may list a familiar brand name. The actual URL may show that the article is on the main news site, a content account, a different subdomain, a partner environment or a regional section.

These are not automatically good or bad. But they are not interchangeable.

## A real URL helps separate publication from recommendation

Some services describe homepage exposure, app recommendation, featured placement or editorial publication.

A live URL confirms publication. It does not necessarily prove that the article appeared on a homepage or was algorithmically recommended.

If recommendation matters, the provider should explain how that exposure can be verified separately.

## URLs are useful for later search checks

After publication, the buyer may want to know whether the article is indexed by Google, Bing, Baidu or other search systems.

The live URL provides a stable reference point for those checks.

## Screenshots are useful, but secondary

Screenshots can document temporary recommendation positions or how a page looked at publication time. But they should not replace a live URL when the page itself is expected to remain publicly accessible.

## What to check when you receive a URL

For each placement, review:

1. Is the domain what you expected?
2. Does the page open publicly?
3. Is the company name correct?
4. Is the headline correct?
5. Is the article content complete?
6. Is the publication date visible?
7. Is the page type consistent with the package description?
8. Can the URL be shared outside China?
9. Is the page still live after the initial publication period?

## Final thought

For China press release distribution, the real publishing URL is one of the most important pieces of evidence.

It answers a simple but essential question: **Where was this actually published?**

PR Channel emphasizes public URLs and page-level verification because media names alone do not always provide enough information.

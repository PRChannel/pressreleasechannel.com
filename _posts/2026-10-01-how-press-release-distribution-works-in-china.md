For overseas companies, press release distribution in China can look simple from the outside: prepare an announcement, choose media and publish it.

In practice, the process has several layers. The quality of the announcement, the relevance of the media, the page type, the editorial review process and the final public URL all affect what the placement actually means.

## Start with the news, not the media list

A useful distribution plan begins with the announcement itself.

Before choosing media, clarify:

- what happened;
- why the development matters;
- who needs to know;
- whether the information is national, regional or industry-specific;
- whether the release contains claims that can be supported.

This matters because media selection should follow the subject and audience. Choosing outlets only because the names are recognizable can produce a list that looks impressive but is poorly matched to the story.

## Choose media by relevance, not by logo alone

A company announcement may be more suitable for business media, industry publications, regional outlets or general news platforms depending on the subject.

A technology launch and a regional factory opening should not automatically use the same media mix.

For overseas companies, a useful media discussion should therefore include more than names. It should also explain the expected publishing environment and why those outlets are relevant to the announcement.

## Understand the publishing environment

A familiar Chinese media brand may appear in different environments.

These can include:

- a main-site news page;
- a regional or local channel;
- a content account;
- a mobile page;
- a special-topic page;
- a recommendation or featured placement.

These formats should not automatically be treated as equivalent.

The actual domain and page type help show what kind of placement has been published.

## Submission still involves editorial review

Press release distribution is not the same as automatic posting.

Depending on the outlet and the content, editors may:

- accept the release;
- request changes;
- edit the headline or body;
- delay publication;
- reject the submission.

Companies should therefore prepare factual, clear and supportable information before distribution.

Claims that are exaggerated, difficult to verify or framed as advertising may make the review process more difficult.

## Verify the live page after publication

Once an article is live, the public URL becomes the most useful verification point.

Check:

- the actual domain;
- the page type;
- whether the article opens publicly;
- the published headline;
- whether the company information is accurate;
- whether the page is still accessible later.

A screenshot can document a moment in time, but it should not replace the live page when a public URL exists.

## Publication is not the same as recommendation

A published article may simply exist as a public page.

That does not automatically mean it was:

- recommended on a homepage;
- featured in an app;
- distributed through a push notification;
- ranked prominently in search.

If recommendation is part of a media offer, it should be described separately from the publication itself.

## Publication, indexing and AI visibility are different

A live article may or may not be indexed by Google or other search engines. It may or may not be retrieved, cited or used by an AI system.

These are different stages:

1. publication;
2. search indexing or retrieval;
3. citation or recommendation;
4. inclusion in a final answer.

Treating all four as one outcome creates unrealistic expectations.

## A practical workflow for overseas companies

A reasonable press release distribution workflow in China is:

1. confirm the factual announcement;
2. define the audience and geography;
3. prepare a clear press release;
4. select relevant media types;
5. understand the expected page type;
6. submit for editorial review;
7. verify the live URL after publication;
8. evaluate search or AI visibility separately.

The process works best when the company understands what is being published, where it is being published and what the placement can realistically achieve.

Press release distribution is useful when there is real company news that needs public, verifiable media exposure. It is less useful when the objective is simply to accumulate media logos or page counts without considering relevance.

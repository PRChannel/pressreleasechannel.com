Two Chinese media placements can display the same recognizable brand name and still represent different products.

For overseas buyers, this is one of the most important things to understand before comparing media lists.

## The media name is only one layer

A placement should be evaluated at several levels:

- media brand;
- actual domain;
- page type;
- publishing account;
- regional or national scope;
- recommendation status.

The brand name alone cannot answer all of these questions.

## Main-site news pages

A main-site page is hosted within the media organization's primary web environment or an official section of that environment.

These pages are often what overseas clients imagine when they see a familiar media name on a media list.

But even within a main site, different channels or sections may have different editorial purposes.

## Content accounts

Many media organizations or publishing teams also operate accounts on larger content platforms.

A content-account page may show a recognizable publisher name, but the technical environment is different from the publisher's own main website.

That does not automatically make the page invalid.

It simply means it should be described accurately.

When comparing placements, ask:

- What is the actual domain?
- Is the page on the publisher's own site?
- Is it an account hosted by another platform?
- How is the publisher identity displayed?

## Regional and local pages

A national media brand may also operate regional or local channels.

These can be useful when the announcement is geographically relevant.

However, a regional page should not automatically be described as identical to a national editorial placement.

Geography is part of the placement.

## Recommendation pages

Recommendation is a separate layer from publication.

An article can be publicly available without being recommended on:

- a homepage;
- an app;
- a channel page;
- a featured module.

If recommendation is sold as part of the package, ask how it can be verified.

Recommendation can also be time-sensitive. A page that was featured for several hours may later exist only as a normal article page.

## Mobile and app environments

Some media products are designed primarily for mobile or app consumption.

These may not look the same as desktop web pages.

If an overseas client needs a publicly verifiable URL, ask whether the placement can be opened in a normal browser and whether the page can be accessed without a specific app.

## Why screenshots are not enough

A screenshot can show that something appeared at a certain moment, but it may not explain:

- the real domain;
- the page type;
- whether the content is still accessible;
- whether the screenshot represents publication or recommendation.

A live URL is usually more useful for long-term verification.

## The better question

Do not ask only:

> “Is this a big media name?”

Ask:

> **“What type of placement am I actually getting?”**

That question is more useful for pricing, verification and long-term record keeping.

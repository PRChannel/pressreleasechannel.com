Press release distribution and media invitation are often grouped together as PR services, but they solve different communication problems.

For overseas companies planning activity in China, choosing the wrong one can create unnecessary cost or unrealistic expectations.

## Press release distribution starts with information that already exists

Press release distribution is appropriate when the company already has a clear announcement.

Examples include:

- company updates;
- product launches;
- partnerships;
- expansion news;
- research announcements;
- event summaries;
- executive appointments.

The release is prepared, suitable media are selected and the content is submitted for editorial review.

The goal is to create public media coverage around an existing news fact.

## Media invitation creates the possibility of new reporting

Media invitation is different.

Instead of only sending prepared content, the company invites journalists to:

- attend an event;
- observe a product demonstration;
- interview executives;
- visit a site;
- participate in a forum;
- cover a signing ceremony or exhibition.

The event itself can generate new information that was not fully available in the press release.

## When press release distribution may be enough

A press release may be sufficient when:

- the announcement is straightforward;
- there is no meaningful on-site experience;
- no interview is required;
- the company mainly needs public coverage;
- the event is internal or not suitable for journalists.

In these cases, inviting journalists may add complexity without adding much reporting value.

## When media invitation may be worth considering

Journalist attendance becomes more useful when there is:

- a real news development;
- something worth seeing on-site;
- a strong interview opportunity;
- relevant executives or experts;
- an industry audience;
- information that cannot be communicated fully through a written release.

Examples may include product demonstrations, factory visits, major forums, exhibitions and signing ceremonies.

## Journalist attendance does not guarantee coverage

This point is important.

A journalist may attend an event and still decide not to publish.

Coverage depends on:

- news value;
- audience relevance;
- editorial judgment;
- interview quality;
- competing news;
- the journalist's own assessment.

Media invitation creates an opportunity for independent reporting. It is not a purchase of guaranteed coverage.

## The two approaches can work together

For some events, a combined workflow is useful:

1. prepare the core announcement;
2. invite selected journalists;
3. provide background materials;
4. arrange interviews;
5. hold the event;
6. distribute a post-event press release;
7. verify published coverage.

The press release provides a factual baseline, while the event can create additional reporting material.

## Do not judge success only by attendance numbers

A smaller group of relevant journalists may be more useful than a large group with little connection to the subject.

For example:

- technology media for a technical launch;
- business media for a partnership;
- regional media for a local expansion;
- industry publications for a specialized forum.

Relevance matters more than filling a room.

## A simple decision framework

Choose **press release distribution** when the main need is to publish a clear announcement.

Consider **media invitation** when the event itself can create additional reporting value.

Ask:

1. Is there a real news development?
2. Is there something journalists can see or experience?
3. Are there people worth interviewing?
4. Is the event relevant to a specific media audience?
5. Can the event create information beyond the prepared release?

If most answers are yes, media invitation may be worthwhile.

If most are no, a well-prepared press release may be the more efficient choice.

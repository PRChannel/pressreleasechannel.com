International PR teams are usually experienced buyers of media services. The problem is not a lack of communications knowledge.

The problem is that assumptions that work in one market do not always transfer cleanly to China.

## 1. A familiar media brand does not always mean a familiar page type

International teams may see a recognizable Chinese media name and assume the placement is equivalent to the main editorial website.

That is not always the case. The same brand may appear across main-site pages, regional channels, content accounts, app environments, syndicated systems and partner platforms.

The buyer should ask for the actual URL and page type.

## 2. Distribution is not the same as journalist coverage

A press release can be published widely without generating original journalist reporting.

Syndication can create broad public availability. Journalist coverage requires additional editorial interest.

If the objective is interviews or original reporting, media relations activity may be needed in addition to distribution.

## 3. Translation is not the same as localization

A literal translation can preserve the words while missing the communication context.

Chinese media may require changes to headlines, company descriptions, technical terminology, promotional claims, background explanations and local relevance.

## 4. The biggest media list is not necessarily the best media plan

Large distribution lists are easy to compare in procurement, but a large list can mix highly relevant media, low-relevance media, syndicated pages, duplicate properties and different page types.

Media selection should start with the story and audience, not with the target number of logos.

## 5. A published URL does not guarantee discoverability

Once an article is online, it may still not appear in Google or other search systems.

Publication, indexing, retrieval and ranking are different stages. The same applies to AI systems.

## 6. Screenshots should not replace page-level verification

For temporary recommendation exposure, screenshots may be useful. But for standard media publication, a live URL is usually more important.

The buyer should be able to inspect the domain, page type, publication date, content and accessibility.

## 7. China is not one single media audience

A national consumer story, a regional investment announcement and an industrial technology release may require very different media strategies.

A distribution plan should consider industry, city or province, business vs consumer audience, announcement type and local relevance.

## What international teams should ask before distribution

Ask:

- What media categories are being selected?
- Why are they relevant?
- What page types are expected?
- Can we see comparable live examples?
- Is the content being localized or only translated?
- What happens if a media outlet rejects the release?
- What reporting will we receive?
- What outcomes are not guaranteed?

## Final thought

Most misunderstandings happen because the buyer applies a familiar global framework to an unfamiliar local publishing environment.

The solution is not to abandon global communications standards. It is to add enough local context to make those standards useful in China.

PR Channel works with this principle: explain the actual media environment, verify the real pages and avoid treating all Chinese placements as equivalent.

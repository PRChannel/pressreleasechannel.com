No. Press release distribution in China does not automatically guarantee publication on every selected media outlet.

This is an important distinction because “distribution” describes the submission and placement process, while the final publication decision may still depend on editorial review.

## Why publication can be rejected

A media outlet may reject a press release for many reasons.

Common examples include:

- the topic is not relevant to the outlet;
- the release is too promotional;
- important claims cannot be supported;
- the content conflicts with editorial rules;
- the subject falls into a restricted category;
- the timing is unsuitable;
- the media has changed its publishing policy.

A provider can help with media selection and submission, but it cannot remove every editorial rule.

## Editing is also part of the process

Even when a release is accepted, the final page may not be identical to the submitted document.

Editors may change:

- the headline;
- sentence structure;
- formatting;
- image placement;
- company boilerplate;
- wording that appears too promotional.

Companies with legally sensitive wording should identify those sections before submission.

## “Guaranteed publication” needs context

Some publishing products may be sold with a high level of certainty, but overseas companies should ask what type of publishing environment that guarantee refers to.

For example:

- Is it a main-site editorial page?
- Is it a content account?
- Is it a partner or syndicated page?
- Is it a self-publishing environment?
- Does the outlet retain the right to reject certain content?

The phrase “guaranteed publication” is not useful without this context.

## Publication is different from recommendation

Even when an article is published, that does not automatically mean it will receive:

- homepage recommendation;
- app recommendation;
- prominent channel placement;
- large readership;
- social distribution.

Those are separate outcomes.

If recommendation is included in a package, the method of verification should be explained.

## Publication is also different from permanent availability

A page that is live today may not remain online forever.

Media websites can:

- redesign pages;
- remove old content;
- change URL structures;
- close sections;
- apply new content rules.

Where a provider offers an explicit support period, that period should be understood before purchase.

PR Channel's policy is that where a resource has an explicit support period, that period applies; otherwise the default support period is one month.

## Search and AI visibility are not guaranteed either

A published page does not automatically guarantee:

- Google indexing;
- search ranking;
- inclusion in an AI retrieval system;
- citation by an AI system;
- recommendation in a generated answer.

These systems make their own decisions after publication.

## What can reasonably be expected?

A clearer expectation is:

1. the announcement is reviewed;
2. suitable media are selected;
3. the release is submitted;
4. editorial feedback is handled where possible;
5. accepted placements are published;
6. live URLs are verified.

That is a more realistic description of press release distribution than a promise that every outlet will publish every release.

## What should companies ask before paying?

Ask:

- Is editorial review required?
- What happens if a selected outlet rejects the release?
- Can another suitable outlet be considered?
- What page type is expected?
- Will I receive a public URL?
- What support period applies after publication?
- Are recommendation, indexing or AI visibility being sold separately?

Clear answers to these questions make the service easier to evaluate.

The absence of a publication guarantee is not necessarily a weakness. In many cases, it reflects the fact that independent media retain editorial control.

When an overseas company buys press release distribution in China, the invoice may look simple. The service may be described as “China press release distribution.” But that single line can hide several very different types of work.

If a buyer does not separate those parts, it becomes difficult to compare providers, understand pricing or judge whether the final result is useful.

## 1. Media selection

The first part is not publication. It is deciding where the release should go.

A provider may select media based on industry, location, company type, announcement topic, target audience, editorial requirements and expected page type.

Good media selection is not simply choosing the largest names. It is choosing placements that fit the announcement.

## 2. Translation and localization

Some overseas companies already have a Chinese release. Others only have an English announcement.

The service may include translation, terminology adjustment, headline rewriting, reduction of promotional language, company-description adjustments and checking names, titles and product terms.

These are separate tasks. A provider that only translates the release is not necessarily doing the same work as a provider that localizes it for Chinese media review.

## 3. Editorial submission

Press release distribution does not always mean automatic publishing.

The provider may need to submit the release, answer editor questions, make requested revisions, adjust the headline, remove unsupported claims, resubmit after changes or replace a rejected media option where appropriate.

A lower price may sometimes reflect a more automated syndication model with less manual editorial handling. A higher price may include more human coordination.

## 4. The actual media placement

This is the part most buyers think they are paying for. But “a media placement” is not a single standardized object.

It may be an article on a media website, a regional channel page, a content account, a syndicated copy, a mobile page or another publisher-hosted format.

This is why the actual URL matters. A media list is only a planned destination. The published page is the real deliverable.

## 5. Publication verification

After publication, someone needs to confirm that the URL works, the domain is correct, the title is correct, the page type matches the description and the result can be independently checked.

For overseas teams, this is especially important because the buyer may not be familiar with Chinese domains or publishing environments.

## 6. Reporting

A basic report may contain media name, URL and publication date.

A stronger report may also explain page type, media category, whether the placement is editorial or syndicated, and whether the page is publicly accessible.

The reporting layer matters because a list of 100 links without context may be difficult for an overseas buyer to interpret.

## 7. Support after publication

Published pages are not necessarily permanent. Media websites can redesign sections, change content systems, remove old pages or update policies.

A provider may offer a defined support period after publication. This is different from guaranteeing permanent availability.

Buyers should ask what support is included if a link stops working shortly after publication.

## 8. Account management and international overhead

Some companies are also paying for international account management, procurement compatibility, centralized billing, compliance review, cross-market coordination, multilingual support and enterprise reporting systems.

These services can be useful, especially for larger organizations. But a company focused only on China should understand whether it needs those layers.

## 9. What you should not assume you are paying for

Publication does not automatically guarantee homepage recommendation, journalist-written feature coverage, long-term search rankings, Google indexing, Baidu indexing, AI citation, traffic, leads or sales.

These outcomes may happen, but they should not be treated as automatic consequences of publication.

## A better way to compare quotes

Do not compare only “$X for 50 media vs $Y for 100 media.” Compare media type, actual domains, page types, localization, editorial handling, verification, reporting, support period and campaign relevance.

## Final thought

China press release distribution is not one product. It is a combination of content preparation, media selection, editorial submission, publication, verification, reporting and support.

Different providers bundle those elements differently. The best buyer is not the one who finds the lowest cost per placement. It is the one who understands what each placement represents and which parts of the service are useful for the campaign.

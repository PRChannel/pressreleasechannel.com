There is no single standard price for press release distribution in China.

The cost can vary significantly because “press release distribution” can describe very different services: one media placement, a small targeted list, a broader distribution package, writing or translation support, or a campaign that also includes journalist coordination.

For overseas companies, the useful question is not only **“How much does it cost?”** but also **“What exactly is included in the price?”**

## Media type is one of the biggest cost factors

Different media environments have different editorial requirements, audiences and commercial structures.

A quote may include:

- business media;
- industry publications;
- regional media;
- general news platforms;
- content-account placements;
- special-topic pages;
- other publishing environments.

Two quotes with the same number of placements can therefore represent very different media mixes.

A lower-cost package is not automatically worse, and a higher-cost package is not automatically better. The comparison only becomes meaningful when the media type and page type are clear.

## One placement and a distribution package are not the same product

Some companies only need one or two specific placements.

Others need a broader mix of relevant media.

The pricing model may therefore be:

- per placement;
- by selected media list;
- by distribution package;
- by campaign scope.

When comparing providers, ask whether the quote is based on specific outlets or simply on a promised number of published pages.

## Writing, editing and translation can change the price

If the press release is already complete and suitable for Chinese media review, distribution may be the main cost.

If additional work is needed, the quote may also include:

- English-to-Chinese translation;
- rewriting for a Chinese audience;
- headline adjustment;
- fact checking;
- formatting;
- image handling;
- communication with the client during revision.

These should be separated from media placement costs when possible so the client understands what is being purchased.

## Media invitation is usually a different cost category

Journalist attendance at an event should not be treated as the same service as online press release distribution.

Media invitation may involve:

- identifying relevant journalists;
- invitation and follow-up;
- event coordination;
- interview scheduling;
- on-site communication;
- travel or logistical requirements.

If a package combines online distribution and journalist attendance, the two parts should be explained separately.

## Page type matters more than a simple media count

A quote that says “50 media placements” does not tell you enough.

Before comparing prices, ask:

1. What types of media are included?
2. What type of page will be published?
3. Are the placements on main sites, regional pages, content accounts or other environments?
4. Will public URLs be provided?
5. Is any recommendation or featured exposure included?
6. What support period applies after publication?

Without these answers, comparing only the total price can be misleading.

## Very low prices often require closer inspection

Low-cost distribution can be useful when the objective is broad basic coverage, but overseas companies should still understand the publishing environment.

Check whether the offer relies heavily on:

- duplicated or similar sites;
- content accounts;
- pages that are difficult to verify;
- media labels that are broader than the actual page;
- recommendation claims that cannot be reproduced.

The issue is not that low-cost media are automatically invalid. The issue is whether the buyer understands what the placement actually is.

## Ask for a comparable recent example

Before paying for a larger package, ask for a recent example that is similar to the placement being offered.

A useful example can help verify:

- the real domain;
- the page structure;
- the media label;
- whether the URL is public;
- whether the provider's description matches the page.

This is often more useful than relying on a price sheet alone.

## A practical way to compare quotes

Instead of comparing only price, use a simple framework:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What media types are included? | Shows the actual distribution mix |
| What page types are included? | Helps distinguish main-site, account and other placements |
| Is editorial review required? | Clarifies whether publication is automatic |
| Are public URLs provided? | Makes the result independently verifiable |
| Is recommendation included? | Separates publication from featured exposure |
| What support period applies? | Clarifies what happens if a page later changes or disappears |
| Are writing or translation fees separate? | Makes the real service cost easier to understand |

## So, what should a company budget?

The answer depends on the objective.

A company that needs one specific business publication has a different budget from a company that needs broad multi-industry exposure. A product launch with journalist attendance is also different from a simple corporate announcement.

That is why PR Channel does not treat a media count as a complete description of value.

A useful quote should explain **what is being distributed, where it may be published, what type of placement is expected and what is not guaranteed**.

For overseas companies, that level of clarity is more useful than a single “China press release distribution price” presented without context.
